# Incident response — draft operating procedure

Incident commander, security responder, privacy/controller decision-maker, communications lead, deputies and out-of-hours contacts: **PENDING named assignment**. Contact channel, escalation coverage and exercise date: **PENDING**. This procedure has not been approved or exercised. Do not treat successful CI as incident readiness.

1. **Recognize and record.** Suspected cross-organization access, leaked credential, exposed artifact, unexpected provider disclosure, unavailable export/erasure or missed retention becomes a restricted case. Record detection time in UTC, reporter/channel, affected service/commit and facts versus assumptions. Use an opaque case ID in the repo; no member content or credentials.
2. **Escalate and assess.** Reach the designated security and privacy leads through the approved channel once assigned. Determine confidentiality, integrity and availability impact, likely subjects/data, time window, scope and continuing exposure. Record earliest organizational awareness and uncertainty; do not wait for perfect root-cause evidence before notification assessment.
3. **Contain.** Authorized operators can restrict affected access, suspend an unsafe integration, disable AI via `AI_PROCESSING_ENABLED` or stop a problematic sweep. Preserve the configured release gate. Rotate/revoke compromised credentials through provider tooling; evaluate active sessions and OAuth grants. Rotating the calendar encryption key without a migration/reconnection plan can make stored tokens unreadable. Record who authorized each action and its time.
4. **Preserve evidence safely.** Restrict relevant provider/audit logs, run/deployment identifiers and configuration snapshots in the case system. Use least privilege, access logging and a defined retention/legal-hold decision. Never upload raw production responses, traces, CVs, tokens or member screenshots to Actions or a public issue. If an artifact/log leaked data, restrict access, remove the exposed copy through authorized tooling, rotate affected credentials and assess downstream downloads; deletion alone is not containment proof.
5. **Assess reporting and communication.** The privacy/controller lead determines applicable jurisdictions, reporting duties, deadlines, supervisory authority, individual communications and any justified exceptions with qualified advice. Record the decision, awareness timestamp, rationale and delivery evidence in the restricted case. No universal legal clock or preapproved notification conclusion is asserted here. Pending owner/contact assignment is a readiness gap and must be resolved before reliance on this procedure.
6. **Recover and validate.** Apply reviewed remediation; run synthetic authorization, erasure/export and affected flow checks against the candidate commit. Restore only into restricted isolation, reapply erasure/suppression records and confirm no unintended emails/calendar actions before reopening. Record deployment, gate outcomes and recovery authorization. Do not promise recovery time until RTO/RPO and drills are established.
7. **Close and improve.** Record root cause, affected scope, notifications/decision, residual risk, remediation owner/due date and lessons. Revisit provider access, logs, tests, notices and retention. Keep a sanitized closure entry in the evidence ledger and case details separately.

Minimum case fields: opaque ID; detected/aware/contained/recovered timestamps; impact/unknowns; services and commit/deployment IDs; evidence custody/access; decisions and authorizers; communication recipients and timestamps (restricted); recovery checks; remaining actions. First tabletop should include cross-org exposure, an exposed CI artifact and deletion resurrected by restore. Schedule and owners remain pending.
